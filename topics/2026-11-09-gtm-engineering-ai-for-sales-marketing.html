GTM engineering: AI for sales, marketing, and revenue workflows

GTM used to be campaigns and hero reps. GTM engineering treats revenue like a system: watch for a buyer changing state, enrich and score that signal, route it in minutes, and let AI draft the personalised next step — with a human owning the send. The win is not more messages. It is the right play, triggered by real data, measured like software.

02 The one-idea version

The buyer journey is a state machine, and every GTM problem is a data problem. A company is not “a lead” forever — it moves: unaware → visiting → trialling → expanding → at risk. Clay’s GTM team builds exactly on this: name the states, find the signals that mark a transition (a hiring post, a second trial signup, a support ticket asking about enterprise features), and automate a specific, personalised response to that transition. AI does research, enrichment, and drafting at scale; humans keep judgment, relationships, and the final send.

03 The picture: signal to revenue play

One play, end to end. The same loop runs for outbound, inbound, trial conversion, and expansion — only the signal and the play change.

04 How it works

Six mechanics: state machine first, data before AI, signals, plays, grounded copilots, and engineering-grade measurement.

1

Model the journey as a state machine before you automate anything

List the states a buyer actually moves through for your product — e.g. target account → engaged → trial → activated → customer → expanding → at risk — and for each transition write the observable signal and the one play that should fire. This is Clay’s first GTME principle. If you cannot name the state change, you do not have a play; you have a campaign with extra tooling.

2

Fix the data foundation — AI amplifies whatever data it touches

Dedupe accounts, define your ICP in fields (not in a slide), normalise titles/industries, and keep CRM hygiene continuous. Then enrich with a waterfall: try provider A, fall back to B, then C, paying only on a hit, and use an AI research agent (Claygent-style) for the unstructured residue — does this company use a competitor, what did its 10-K say, who just joined. Bad data in, confidently wrong outreach out — at 10× the volume.

3

Build a signal layer, and score fit × intent separately

Collect first-party signals (pricing-page visits, trial signups, product usage, support conversations), second-party intent, and public triggers (hiring for relevant roles, funding, tech-stack changes, leadership moves). Score two axes independently: fit (is this our ICP?) and intent (are they moving now?). Route only where both clear a threshold; a perfect-fit account with no signal gets nurture, a high-intent poor-fit account gets a polite no — not SDR time.

4

Orchestrate plays, not blasts: trigger → enrich → score → route → draft

Each play is a small workflow with an owner and an SLA: e.g. “2+ trial signups from one company in 7 days” → enrich the company, pick the highest-grade decision-maker, pull prior call context, draft outreach referencing the team’s actual usage, route to the account owner in Slack/CRM within minutes. Inbound speed matters the same way: automated routing and first response in minutes, not the next business day, is itself a conversion lever.

5

Ground the AI in the CRM, and keep humans on every external send

Use the copilot patterns the platforms now ship: Dynamics 365 Sales Copilot summarises records, prepares meetings, and drafts email from the records the signed-in seller can access; HubSpot Breeze summarises records and generates content across sales/marketing/service; Salesforce Agentforce SDR qualifies and books within configured topics and actions. The pattern to copy: generation is grounded in CRM + approved content, permissions are inherited, and a human approves, edits, or rejects anything customer-facing. Autonomy inside the CRM; approval at the boundary.

6

Measure each play like a product feature, and iterate or kill it

Instrument per play, not per channel: signals fired, enrichment hit rate, drafts approved vs edited vs rejected, reply rate, meetings, pipeline, and cost per outcome (credits, tokens, rep minutes). Run one change at a time, compare against the previous version, and expect a tuning period — the Cylindo AI-SDR rebuild in the video below took months of segmentation/message/offer iteration before replies jumped. A play with no owner, no metric, and no kill criterion will decay into spam infrastructure.

05 Code sketch

A minimal play engine: a signal arrives, a waterfall enriches it, fit × intent decides routing, and AI drafts — but never sends.

from dataclasses import dataclass

@dataclass
class Signal:
    account: str; kind: str          # "trial_signup", "hiring", "pricing_visit"
    strength: float                  # 0..1, set by the detector

def waterfall(account, providers):
    # Try each provider in order; pay / stop on first hit
    for p in providers:
        data = p.enrich(account)
        if data: return data
    return {}

def run_play(sig: Signal, crm, providers, llm):
    acct = waterfall(sig.account, providers)
    fit = icp_fit(acct)                       # fields, not vibes: size, industry, stack
    intent = sig.strength
    if fit < 0.6 or intent < 0.5:
        crm.log(sig, outcome="nurture"); return "nurture"
    owner = crm.owner_of(sig.account)         # route in minutes, with context
    context = crm.history(sig.account)        # calls, emails, usage — what seller can see
    draft = llm.draft(                        # grounded draft, NOT a send
        template="trial_team_expansion", account=acct, context=context)
    crm.create_task(owner, draft=draft, sla_minutes=30,
                    requires_approval=True)  # human approves / edits / rejects
    crm.log(sig, outcome="routed", fit=fit, intent=intent)
    return "routed"

06 Failure modes

Five ways AI GTM goes wrong in production — each with a one-line fix.

1

Automating a dirty CRMFix: dedupe, normalise, and define ICP in fields first; add a continuous hygiene play before any outreach play.

Enrichment and AI drafting on stale, duplicated records do not create pipeline — they create wrong-person emails, merged-account errors, and reps who stop trusting the system within a week.

2

The AI SDR spam cannonFix: cap volume by signal quality, require a real trigger per message, and measure replies and meetings per play — never sends.

When drafting gets cheap, the temptation is volume: thousands of “personalised” emails with a first name and a funding headline swapped in. Buyers learn to ignore the pattern, domains burn, and the brand pays for the pipeline team’s metric.

3

Bolt-on AI on an unbuilt processFix: redesign the play first (states, signals, owner, SLA), then choose the tool — expect a tuning period measured in weeks.

Buying an agent for a workflow nobody has defined produces demos, not revenue. The teams in the references got results only after rebuilding segmentation, routing, and the offer underneath the AI.

4

Ungrounded, unapproved external sendsFix: ground drafts in CRM records the rep can access, cite the source facts, and require human approval for every customer-facing send.

An agent that invents a customer’s stack, quotes a price, or emails a prospect with no human review turns a hallucination into a legal and brand incident. Least-privilege data access is not optional here — it is the design.

5

Vanity metrics and set-and-forget playsFix: give every play an owner, a per-play outcome metric, a review cadence, and a kill criterion; log every outcome back to the CRM.

Dashboards full of emails sent and credits burned hide the only question that matters: did this play create qualified pipeline per dollar and rep-hour? Without write-back, scoring never improves and dead plays run forever.

08 Sample prompts

Copy one into your AI assistant and apply it to your own work.

DESIGN A GTM PLAY

Design one GTM engineering play for my product: [product, ICP, average deal size]. Start from a state change, not a campaign. Give me: (1) the trigger signal and how we detect it, (2) the enrichment waterfall (which fields, which provider order, AI research for what), (3) fit and intent scoring thresholds for routing vs nurture, (4) the AI-drafted action and exactly what a human must approve, (5) CRM write-back fields, and (6) the per-play metrics and kill criterion. Flag any step that assumes data we probably do not have yet.

AUDIT MY CRM FOR AI READINESS

Audit my CRM before we add AI GTM tooling. Here is my current state: [CRM, record counts, known duplicate / missing-field problems, how ICP is defined today, enrichment tools if any]. Produce a pre-AI cleanup plan ordered by damage done if skipped: dedupe rules, fields to normalise, ICP-as-fields definition, missing enrichment, and a continuous hygiene workflow. For each item, say which GTM play it blocks. Do not recommend any outreach automation until you list what must be fixed first.

REVIEW AN AI OUTREACH PLAY

Review this AI outreach play like a sceptical RevOps lead: [paste the trigger, audience, draft template, volume, and current reply / meeting rates]. Check: is there a real signal per recipient or is this a blast in disguise? Is every factual claim in the draft grounded in a named source field? Where can a hallucination reach a customer? Is a human approving sends? Rewrite the play with a tighter trigger, a lower volume cap, grounded personalisation, and the metrics that would justify scaling it — or killing it.

10 Flashcards

Click a card (or focus it and press Enter / Space) to flip it.

GTM engineering
Building revenue as a system: engineers (or engineer-minded operators) who design signal detection, enrichment, routing, and AI-assisted plays for sales, marketing, and success — and measure them like software.
State machine (journey)
Modelling the buyer journey as discrete states with observable transition signals. The GTME job: detect the transition and fire the right play automatically. Clay’s first principle.
Signal
An observable event suggesting a state change or buying readiness: pricing-page visit, second trial signup, relevant hiring post, usage spike, support ticket asking about enterprise features.
Waterfall enrichment
Querying data providers in order for a field, stopping on the first hit so you pay only for found data. AI research agents handle unstructured questions a database cannot answer.
Fit vs intent
Fit = how well an account matches the ICP (size, industry, stack). Intent = evidence they are moving now. Route where both are high; nurture high-fit / low-intent.
Play
A triggered workflow with an owner and an SLA: signal → enrich → score → route → AI draft → human send → CRM write-back. The unit of GTM engineering, replacing the one-off campaign.
AI SDR
An agent that researches accounts, qualifies leads, and drafts (or, in some products, sends) outreach and books meetings. Works only on clean data, a real signal per recipient, and a tuned offer.
Grounded copilot
CRM copilots (Dynamics Copilot, HubSpot Breeze, Agentforce) that summarise and draft from records the user can access, inheriting permissions — generation inside the system of record, approval at the boundary.
RevOps
Revenue operations: the team owning the CRM, routing, scoring, and measurement that GTM engineering builds on. In an AI GTM stack, RevOps owns the data model and the play metrics.

11 Reach for this when…

Reps are doing research robots should do — pre-call account research, lead enrichment, routing, and first-draft outreach consume the week. Automate the research and drafting; keep humans on judgment and relationships.
Signals exist but nobody acts in time — trial signups, pricing visits, and support intent sit in dashboards until they are stale. Build the play: detect, enrich, route in minutes, with an SLA.
You are evaluating AI sales/marketing tools — ask first whether your states, signals, CRM data, and per-play metrics exist. If they do not, the engineering work comes before the purchase, whatever the demo promises.
