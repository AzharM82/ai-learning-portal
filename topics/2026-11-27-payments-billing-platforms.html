Payments and billing platforms: ledgers, invoicing, and reconciliation

Money systems fail differently: a retry double-charges, a timeout leaves a payment in limbo, and three systems each claim a different truth. This lesson is the core that survives all of that — an immutable double-entry ledger, invoices as a state machine, idempotency everywhere, and reconciliation as a daily job, not a month-end panic.

02 The one-idea version

Never store a balance you update — store what happened, and derive the balance. A mutable balance = balance - 100 destroys the only record that can explain a disagreement later. An append-only double-entry ledger keeps it: every transaction posts at least two entries that sum to zero, balances are a sum over entries, and a mistake is fixed by a reversing entry, never by editing history. Invoices, payments, and reconciliation are then just views and checks on top of that one truth.

03 The picture: invoice, ledger, reconciliation

An invoice finalizes into ledger entries; money moves against it; reconciliation proves the ledger, the provider, and the bank agree.

04 How it works

Six mechanics: ledger first, then invoicing, idempotency, and the reconciliation loop.

1

Post double-entry, append-only — and check the sum in the database

Model a transaction as a header plus entries: each entry is one account, a debit or credit, an amount in integer minor units (cents, never floats), and a currency. Enforce in one DB transaction that total debits = total credits per currency, then insert — never update or delete an entry. Stripe does exactly this underneath: finalizing an invoice debits AccountsReceivable and credits DeferredRevenue; paying it debits Cash and credits AccountsReceivable. A balance is SUM(entries) for an account, optionally snapshotted for speed and re-derived to verify the snapshot.

2

Give every account a type and a normal side

Keep a small chart of accounts: assets (Cash, Clearing, AccountsReceivable), liabilities (DeferredRevenue, CustomerBalance, TaxPayable), revenue, and contra/expense accounts (Refunds, Fees, BadDebt, FX loss). Stripe’s revenue-recognition chart is a good template. Separate clearing accounts per provider: money authorized is not money settled, and the clearing account is where that gap lives — visibly — until settlement lands.

3

Run invoices as a state machine, not a document you edit

States: draft → open (finalized) → paid, with void and uncollectible as terminal exits. Finalization freezes line items, assigns a gapless invoice number, computes tax, and posts the AR/deferred entries. After finalization, change nothing in place: correct with a credit note (its own ledger transaction), collect with payment attempts recorded against the invoice, and prorate subscription changes as new line items — the pattern Stripe Billing uses for its immutable customer-balance ledger, where a reversal is an opposite transaction, not a deletion.

4

Make every money-moving call idempotent — client key, unique constraint, stored result

Require a client-generated idempotency key for payment creation, capture, refund, and invoice finalization. Insert the key with a unique constraint before calling the provider, in the same transaction as the intent record; on a duplicate, return the original result instead of executing again. Pass the same key through to the provider. Treat provider timeouts as UNKNOWN, not FAILED: query status with the key before any retry. Verify webhook signatures, deduplicate by event ID, and apply webhooks idempotently — they will be redelivered.

5

Separate authorization, capture, and settlement in the payment state machine

Authorize holds funds, capture claims them, settlement is when the bank actually moves them — often days later, batched, net of fees. Model each as an explicit state with allowed transitions, store transitions as events, and never mark an invoice paid on authorization alone. Record fees, taxes, and FX as their own ledger entries at settlement using the settlement file’s actual amounts and rate, so the difference between expected and actual is a number in the ledger, not a surprise at month-end.

6

Reconcile three ways, daily, and queue the breaks

Every day, match: (1) your ledger against the provider’s transaction report, (2) provider settlements against the bank statement / settlement file, and (3) open invoices against incoming transfers. Follow Stripe’s cash-balance order: reference / invoice-number match first, then exact-amount match (single or a small group), then oldest-due. Anything unmatched goes to a suspense account and an exception queue with an owner and an age — unreconciled funds left sitting are a liability, and providers will eventually return or sweep aged balances. Alert on break count and oldest-break age, not just on job failure.

7

Close the period like you mean it

Lock accounting periods: once a period closes, new entries need a dated adjusting entry in the next period, never a backdated edit. Export journal entries (debits/credits report) for accounting, keep the immutable audit trail of who/what created every transaction, and reconcile customer sub-ledgers (invoice balance, billing credits, cash balance) separately — Stripe keeps those three ledgers distinct because mixing them is how credits quietly become cash.

05 Code sketch

A minimal double-entry post in Python: balanced, idempotent, append-only.

# ledger.py — post a balanced transaction or post nothing
import sqlite3

db = sqlite3.connect("ledger.db")
db.execute("CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS entries (txn TEXT, account TEXT, debit INT, credit INT, ccy TEXT, idem TEXT UNIQUE)")

def post(txn_id, idem_key, lines, ccy="USD"):
    # lines: [(account, debit_cents, credit_cents), ...] — integers only
    assert sum(d for _, d, _ in lines) == sum(c for _, _, c in lines), "unbalanced"
    assert all(isinstance(v, int) for _, d, c in lines for v in (d, c))
    try:
        with db:  # one transaction: all entries or none
            db.executemany(
                "INSERT INTO entries VALUES (?,?,?,?,?,?)",
                [(txn_id, a, d, c, ccy, idem_key if i == 0 else None)
                 for i, (a, d, c) in enumerate(lines)])
    except sqlite3.IntegrityError:
        return "duplicate — original result stands"  # idempotent retry
    return "posted"

# Finalize a $100 invoice, then pay it — Stripe's own journal pattern
post("inv_1042-finalize", "fin:inv_1042",
     [("accounts_receivable", 10000, 0), ("deferred_revenue", 0, 10000)])
post("inv_1042-pay", "pay:inv_1042:attempt1",
     [("cash", 10000, 0), ("accounts_receivable", 0, 10000)])
# Balance = derived, never stored: SELECT SUM(debit-credit) ... per account

06 Failure modes

Five ways billing platforms quietly lose money — each with a one-line fix.

!

The mutable balance columnFix: Code updates balance in place; a partial failure or race leaves a number no one can explain. Fix: append-only entries, derived balances, corrections as reversing entries.

!

Retry = double chargeFix: A timeout is retried with a new request and the customer pays twice. Fix: client idempotency key + unique constraint + stored intent before the provider call; on timeout, query status — UNKNOWN is not FAILED.

!

Editing a finalized invoiceFix: Support ‘fixes’ the total after finalization and the books no longer match what the customer saw. Fix: freeze on finalize; correct with a credit note and a new ledger transaction.

!

Floats and silent roundingFix: Amounts in binary floating point drift by cents that compound across millions of entries. Fix: integer minor units everywhere, one explicit rounding rule per currency, FX differences posted to their own account.

!

Reconciliation as a month-end spreadsheetFix: Breaks age for weeks, unmatched transfers pile up in a customer balance, and no one owns them. Fix: daily three-way reconciliation, breaks in a suspense queue with owner + age alerts.

10 Flashcards

Click a card (or focus it and press Enter / Space) to flip it.

Double-entry bookkeeping
Every transaction posts at least two entries — a debit and a credit — that sum to zero per currency. Money never appears or disappears; it moves between accounts, which is what makes the books provable.
Append-only ledger
Entries are inserted, never updated or deleted. A mistake is corrected by a reversing entry plus a new correct entry, so the full story — including the mistake — stays auditable.
Minor units
Store amounts as integers in the currency’s smallest unit (cents for USD). No floats, no binary rounding drift; define one explicit rounding rule where a calculation must round.
Clearing account
The account where money sits between authorization / capture and actual bank settlement. Its non-zero balance is the visible, expected gap — until settlement entries clear it.
Deferred revenue
A liability: cash (or a receivable) for service not yet delivered. It converts to Revenue only as the service is delivered — which is why finalize credits Deferred, not Revenue.
Invoice finalization
The moment a draft becomes a real bill: line items freeze, a number is assigned, tax is computed, and the AR / deferred-revenue entries post. After this point, corrections are credit notes.
Credit note
A separate document that reduces what a customer owes on a finalized invoice (or funds a refund / balance credit). It carries its own ledger transaction and links back to the original invoice.
Idempotency key
A client-generated unique key per intended money movement, stored with a unique constraint before any provider call. A retry with the same key returns the original result instead of moving money twice.
Reconciliation break
Any item that does not match across ledger, provider, and bank records. Breaks belong in a suspense / exception queue with an owner and an age — never in a spreadsheet, never auto-written-off.
UNKNOWN is not FAILED
After a timeout you do not know whether the provider acted. The only safe move is a status query keyed by your idempotency key. Retrying blind is how double charges are born.

11 Reach for this when…

You are about to store money as a mutable number anywhere — stop and design the ledger first; every later feature (refunds, credits, payouts) is a ledger transaction or it is a bug waiting for month-end.
Customers report double charges, or finance cannot tie payouts to invoices — audit idempotency (keys, constraints, webhook dedupe) and run the three-way reconciliation; the breaks list is your bug list.
You are adding subscriptions, usage billing, credits, or multi-currency — prorations, credit grants, and FX all become explicit ledger entries and separate sub-ledgers, or they will silently mix and drift.
