Linda Raschke’s four setups

Turtle Soup, 80-20’s, the Holy Grail, and the Taylor 3-day cycle: four patterns, traded for decades, that all share one instinct — fade the extreme or buy the first pullback, never chase a move that has already run. Raschke built her career mostly in S&P futures; the patterns transfer to stocks, with care around earnings gaps and single-name news. Everything below is paraphrased in plain language; it is education, not a trading recommendation, and no setup here has been backtested on your universe.

02 The one-idea version

Raschke trades a handful of patterns, deeply, instead of many patterns shallowly. She does her homework the night before — a sheet of roughly a dozen candidates, of which she typically trades only three — and every setup below is either a fade of an extreme or a pullback inside a trend. The edge is not in finding more ideas; it is in executing a few well-understood ones with a stop set before entry and an exit that is a time rule or a trail, never a hope.

04 How it works

Seven mechanics: the rules for each setup, then how day trading, swing trading, and stock trading each change the way you use them. Longs are described; shorts are the mirror image.

1

Turtle Soup — fade a failed 20-day breakout

The original Turtle system bought new 20-day highs. Raschke’s insight: most of those breakouts fail, and the failure itself is the trade.

  • The market makes a new 20-day low, and the previous 20-day low occurred at least 4 trading sessions earlier.
  • After price falls below that prior low, place a buy stop 5–10 ticks (cents, in stocks) above the prior 20-day low. The order is good for that day only.
  • If filled, immediately place a protective sell stop 1 tick below the day’s low.

Exit is time-based: close 2–3 bars after entry, or use a trailing stop. This is a quick trade, not a hold — the edge is the snap-back into the range, and it either happens fast or it doesn’t. Note it openly: this system loses more often than it wins; small wins from many false breakouts must cover the stops, so position sizing and the time exit are load-bearing, not optional.

2

Turtle Soup Plus One — the same setup, one day later

Identical structure, but the trigger comes on the day after the new 20-day low instead of the same day. If the order is not filled on that second day, cancel it — no chasing on day three.

Exits loosen slightly: take partial profits within 2–6 bars and trail the rest. Use Plus One when the breakout day itself closes weakly and you want one extra day of confirmation that the breakdown is failing rather than extending.

3

80-20’s — the next-day reversal after an expansion day

A one-day pattern that sets up the next day’s trade. Signal day (bullish): the market opens in the bottom 20% of its daily range and closes in the top 80% — a strong reversal day that closes near its high.

Entry (next day): if price dips below the signal day’s low and then reverses back above it, buy. The dip that fails is the trigger. Protective stop goes below the dip; trail it from there.

Published research notes the market follows through the next day roughly 80–90% of the time but only closes higher about 50% of the time — so take profits quickly. This is a trade for a fast bounce, not a trend day. The bearish version mirrors: open in the top 20%, close in the bottom 80%, then a next-day rally above the signal high that fails.

4

Holy Grail — the filter is the whole setup

The trend-following setup of the set, and the one with the strictest filter. 14-period ADX must be above 30 and rising — rising for at least two consecutive bars in stricter implementations. Above 30 alone is not enough; the trend must be actively strengthening, not merely strong on paper.

Then wait for the first pullback to the 20-period EMA: the bar’s low touches or goes below the EMA. Only the first pullback after the ADX confirmation counts. A second entry needs ADX to drop below 30 and re-confirm — without that reset, you are no longer trading the Holy Grail, you are trading generic pullbacks, which is a different (and weaker) game.

5

Holy Grail — entry, stop, and exit

  • Entry: buy stop above the high of the bar that touched the EMA.
  • Stop: below the pullback bar’s low (the recent swing low).
  • Exit: ADX dropping below 20 (trend over), a close back across the 20 EMA against the trade, or a trailing stop under the EMA — or profits at recent swing highs.

If the first day gives a nice move, she sometimes holds overnight for a second day — this is the bridge between her day trading and swing trading. Works intraday (15-minute bars and up) and on dailies.

6

Taylor 3-day cycle — the swing-trading framework

From George Douglas Taylor’s “Book Method,” which Raschke has called one of her main concepts. The market moves in a repeating 3-day rhythm:

  • Buy Day: buy the dip. Look for one roughly 2 days after a swing high. The reference price is the previous day’s low; a move below it creates the “excess low” that marks the end of the decline, and the trade is to buy as price rejects lower prices and rallies back above the previous day’s low.
  • Sell Day: the next day — take profits into strength.
  • Sell Short Day: the third day — fade the pullback; it can also become the Buy Day of the next cycle if the correction is deep enough and reverses.

Swing highs and lows are marked by either a “violation” (a test above or below the prior day’s high or low) or a “good gap” (a gap the other way that traps the prior day’s players). The labels are used to anticipate the day’s likely play, not as a rigid count — when the count breaks down, she suspends it and waits for a fresh Buy-Day low to restart it.

7

Day trading vs swing trading — and using them on stocks

All four setups scale across timeframes; what changes is the bar size and the hold. Turtle Soup and the Holy Grail work both ways, 80-20’s is inherently a next-day trade, and the Taylor cycle is a swing framework.

SetupDay trading useSwing trading use
Turtle SoupIntraday bars; exit 2–3 bars after entryDaily bars; same rules, stops scaled to the chart
80-20’sSignal day found on dailies; entry executed intraday the next morningHold the bounce a day or two at most — it is not a trend trade
Holy Grail15-min bars and up; a strong first day may be held overnightDaily bars; the classic form — first pullback in a confirmed trend
Taylor cycleLabels set the day’s bias before the openThe framework itself: Buy Day entry, Sell Day exit, Sell-Short fade

For a stock trader, three adaptations matter. First, she traded futures; stocks gap on earnings and single-name news in ways futures don’t, so any stock version must handle gaps explicitly — an overnight gap through your stop is a different risk than an intraday futures wiggle. Second, keep her buckets separate: day trades, 1–3 day swings, and longer holds are different profit centers with different rules — never let a day trade silently become a swing trade because it is losing. Third, do the homework the night before: pick the candidates in advance, so intraday time is spent executing, not discovering.

05 Code sketch

A tiny illustrative checker: given one day’s bars and a few indicator values, which Raschke setups are even candidates? Real use needs proper ADX/EMA series and gap handling — this is the logic shape, not a scanner.

#!/usr/bin/env python3 — raschke_check.py (illustrative logic, not a scanner)
def turtle_soup(lows, i):
    # new 20-bar low, strictly below every low of the prior 4 bars
    window = lows[max(0, i - 19):i + 1]
    return lows[i] == min(window) and all(lows[i] < x for x in lows[max(0, i - 4):i])

def eighty_twenty(o, h, l, c):
    # bullish signal day: opens bottom 20%, closes top 80% of range
    rng = h - l
    return rng > 0 and (o - l) / rng <= 0.2 and (c - l) / rng >= 0.8

def holy_grail(adx, adx_prev, bar_low, ema20, first_pullback):
    # ADX > 30 AND rising, first pullback touching the 20 EMA only
    return adx > 30 and adx > adx_prev and bar_low <= ema20 and first_pullback

def taylor_label(day_index):
    # labels anticipate; when the count breaks, suspend and re-phase
    return ["Buy Day", "Sell Day", "Sell Short Day"][day_index % 3]

print(eighty_twenty(o=100, h=110, l=99, c=109))  # True: textbook 80-20 signal day
print(taylor_label(0), holy_grail(34, 31, 99.5, 100, True))

06 Failure modes

Five ways these setups go wrong, each with the fix.

!

Chasing the breakout instead of fading itFix: Turtle Soup only exists because breakouts fail — if you buy the new 20-day high with the crowd, you are the liquidity the setup feeds on. Wait for the failure, then take the snap-back.

The instinct to buy strength is strong, and it is exactly backwards here. The setup’s trigger is a buy stop above the old low after price broke below it — a reclaim, not a breakout. Entering on the break itself turns a fade into a momentum chase with no defined edge.

!

Taking the second or third pullback as a Holy GrailFix: first pullback only. After it, the setup is spent until ADX drops below 30 and re-confirms above 30 and rising. A later pullback needs a new confirmation, not your patience.

Every pullback in a strong trend looks tempting, and every one after the first is a different trade: the trend is older, the easy continuation is gone, and the ADX filter that made the first one special no longer describes the situation. Without the first-only rule, the Holy Grail degrades into generic pullback trading.

!

Holding an 80-20 for the trend dayFix: take profits quickly. The market follows through the next day most of the time but only closes higher about half the time — the bounce is real, the trend day is a coin flip.

An 80-20 signal day feels like the start of something big, because the signal day itself was big. The statistics say otherwise: the edge is in the next-day bounce, and holding for a multi-day run gives the edge back.

!

Counting the Taylor cycle rigidlyFix: when the count breaks down, suspend it and wait for a fresh Buy-Day low to restart it. The labels are a hypothesis about today’s play, not a calendar.

Trending markets shred the 3-day rhythm — a strong trend will print Sell Day after Sell Day while the counter fades. Forcing the label onto a trending tape produces Sell-Short trades into a runaway market, which is precisely when fading hurts most.

!

Ignoring stock-specific gapsFix: size for the gap, not the intraday wiggle. A stock can gap through your stop on earnings or single-name news; futures-style stops assume a continuous tape that stocks do not always give you.

These setups were built on futures, where the tape rarely jumps your stop by 8%. On stocks, an earnings gap can turn a tight Turtle Soup stop into a much larger loss before you can act — and a gap can also fake a Taylor “good gap” or an 80-20 signal day. Check the earnings calendar before taking any of the four on a single name.

11 Reach for this when…

A stock breaks to a new 20-day extreme and everyone is piling in — check the Turtle Soup conditions instead: most breakouts fail, and the reclaim of the old extreme is the trade, with a 2–3 bar clock.
A strong trend pulls back to its 20 EMA for the first time since ADX confirmed — that is the Holy Grail window. If it is the second or third pullback, the window has closed; wait for an ADX reset.
You are planning tomorrow’s swing trades tonight — label each candidate’s Taylor day first: Buy Day means plan a dip buy, Sell Day means plan an exit into strength, Sell Short Day means plan a fade — and if the count has broken, plan nothing until a fresh Buy-Day low appears.
